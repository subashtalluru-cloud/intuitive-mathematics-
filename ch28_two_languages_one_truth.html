Two Civilizations, One Truth — Watch Them Meet
Euclid (Greece, ~300 BCE) proved this fact using pure geometric construction. Descartes (France, 1637) proved the identical fact using pure algebra. Neither knew the other's method. Compare both proofs, side by side, of the same claim: the diagonal of a 3-by-4 rectangle is 5.
📐 Euclid's Language Geometry, ~300 BCE
Construct squares on each of the two legs (area 9 and area 16). Construct a square on the hypotenuse. Euclid's own geometric proof (Book I, Proposition 47) shows the two smaller squares' combined AREA exactly fills the larger square — no algebra, no coordinates, purely constructed shapes.
🔢 Descartes's Language Algebra, 1637 CE
1. Place the rectangle's corner at origin (0,0).
2. Opposite corner sits at (3,4) — pure coordinate numbers, no shape drawn.
3. Distance formula: d = √((3−0)² + (4−0)²)
4. d = √(9 + 16) = √25
5. d = 5
Both methods: diagonal = 5. Same truth, zero shared vocabulary.
