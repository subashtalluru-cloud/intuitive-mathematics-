The Trade That Helps vs. the Trade That Hurts
Integral of x*e^x dx. Two possible choices for which factor to call u — LIATE (Algebraic before Exponential) picks correctly. Watch what happens if you pick backward instead.
LIATE-correct: u=x, dv=e^x dx
du = dx (simpler!)
v = e^x
Result: x*e^x - integral(e^x)dx
New integral: integral(e^x)dx - trivially easy
Backward: u=e^x, dv=x dx
du = e^x dx (no simpler)
v = x^2/2
Result: (x^2/2)e^x - integral((x^2/2)e^x)dx
New integral: MORE complicated than the original!
A common mistake is choosing u and dv backward from LIATE, producing an equally or more complicated new integral — the technique's usefulness rests entirely on making a strategic choice, not applying the formula mechanically.
