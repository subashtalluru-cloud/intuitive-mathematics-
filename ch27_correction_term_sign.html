Why the Correction Term Flips Sign at 90°
c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos(C). Drag angle C past and below 90° — watch the triangle's third side physically stretch or shrink exactly as the sign of cos(C) predicts.
When C<90° (acute), cos(C)>0, so the correction term SUBTRACTS — the third side is shorter, because a tighter angle pulls the two known sides closer together. When C>90° (obtuse), cos(C)<0, the correction term ADDS — a wider angle pushes them apart, producing a longer side.
