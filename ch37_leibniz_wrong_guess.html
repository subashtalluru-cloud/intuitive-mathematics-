Even Leibniz Got This Wrong the First Time
f(x)=(x+2)(x^2-1) at x=3. Leibniz's first documented guess: multiply the individual derivatives together. Compare that guess against the correct product rule and a full brute-force expansion.
Leibniz's Wrong First Guess
f'(x) = f1'(x) x f2'(x)
= (1) x (2x)
at x=3: = 1 x 6
Correct Product Rule
f'(x) = f1'(x)f2(x) + f1(x)f2'(x)
= (1)(x^2-1) + (x+2)(2x)
at x=3: = (1)(8) + (5)(6)
An economist relying on the incorrect rule near this point would underestimate the true rate of change by more than a factor of six.
