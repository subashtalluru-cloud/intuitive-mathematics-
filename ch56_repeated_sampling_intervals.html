"95% Confidence" Is About the PROCEDURE, Not This One Interval
The true mean is fixed and never changes (shown as the green line — normally unknown, revealed here only for teaching purposes). Click to draw 20 fresh samples, each producing its own 95% confidence interval. Watch roughly 19 out of 20 capture the true mean — never all 20, and rarely fewer than 18 or 19.
This is the single most persistent misinterpretation in statistics: 95% confidence describes this repeated-sampling PROCEDURE's long-run reliability, never the probability that any one already-computed interval contains the truth.
