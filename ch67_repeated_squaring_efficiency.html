3^10 mod 13 — Computed With Only 4 Small Multiplications
Computing 3^10 directly means multiplying 3 by itself 10 times, then reducing. Repeated squaring gets the same answer with far fewer steps — the exact trick modern cryptography depends on.
The reverse problem — given the result and the base, finding the original exponent — has no known efficient general solution, an asymmetry real cryptographic systems depend on entirely.
