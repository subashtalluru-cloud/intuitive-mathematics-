Infinitely Many Antiderivatives, One Shared Slope Everywhere
f(x)=x has antiderivative F(x)=x^2/2+C for ANY constant C. Drag C and watch the whole curve slide vertically — but its slope at every single point never changes, always matching f(x)=x exactly.
An antiderivative is never unique — any two antiderivatives of the same function differ by exactly a constant, since differentiating a constant always yields zero regardless of its value.
