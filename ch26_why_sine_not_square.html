Why the Power Grid Uses Sine Waves, Not Square Waves
Click to send a sine wave and a square wave through a simulated "electrical component." Watch which one survives undistorted.
A sinusoid is the UNIQUE waveform shape that remains a pure sinusoid, at the identical frequency, after passing through any ideal linear component. A square wave secretly contains many different frequencies mixed together — each affected differently, so the shape comes out distorted. This is precisely why 60Hz (or 50Hz) sine waves, not square waves, power the world's electrical grids.
