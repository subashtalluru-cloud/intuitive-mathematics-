Cantor's Diagonal Argument
Suppose someone claims they've listed every real number between 0 and 1 (rows 1–5 below, an infinite list in principle). Click each highlighted diagonal digit to flip it, building a new number guaranteed to differ from every row.
The new number differs from row 1 in its 1st decimal place, from row 2 in its 2nd, from row 3 in its 3rd — and so on forever. By construction it cannot equal any row on the list, no matter how the list was built or how long it is. So no such complete list can exist: the reals in (0,1) are a strictly larger infinity than the naturals used to number the rows.
